Straw man

Strawman fallacy, Homme de paille

Distorting an opponent’s position, by caricaturing or exaggerating it, in order to refute it more easily.

The essentials

Argument diagram

A holds P.

B restates P as P′, weaker or more extreme.

B refutes P′.

B claims to have refuted P.

Why the argument is invalid

The refutation targets a position that nobody holds. Even if it is correct, it says nothing about the real position.

In practice

Example

A: “We should lower the speed limit on this boulevard.” B: “So you want to ban cars in the city?”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Restate your position precisely and ask the other person to respond to that: “That’s not what I’m saying. I’m saying…”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Red herring

The straw man distorts the opposing position in order to refute it; the red herring changes the subject without even discussing it.

Going further

Source

To avoid it, the principle of charity (Neil L. Wilson, 1959, then Donald Davidson) consists of interpreting what others say in its most coherent version before criticising it.
