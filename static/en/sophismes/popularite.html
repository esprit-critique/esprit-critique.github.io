Appeal to popularity

Argumentum ad populum, Bandwagon fallacy, Appel à la popularité

Considering a claim true or an action good because a large number of people go along with it.

The essentials

Argument diagram

Most people believe P.

So P is true.

Why the argument is invalid

The truth of a claim does not depend on how many people believe it: the majority long believed that the Sun revolved around the Earth. Popularity can be a clue, never proof.

In practice

Example

“Millions of people use homeopathy, it must work.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Ask what evidence supports the claim, regardless of how many people go along with it.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Conformity (bias)

Conformity is a behaviour: we align with the group. The appeal to popularity is an argument: the agreement of the greatest number is presented as proof.

Going further

Source

Douglas Walton, “Appeal to Popular Opinion” (1999): the appeal to popular opinion, classified as a fallacy in most logic textbooks, is not always fallacious, depending on how it is used.
