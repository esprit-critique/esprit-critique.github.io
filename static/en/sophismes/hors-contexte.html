Quoting out of context

Quote mining, Citation hors contexte

Quoting a sentence cut off from its context, so that it seems to say something else, even the opposite, of what its author meant.

The essentials

Argument diagram

The author says: “X, but in reality Y.”

We quote: “X.”

Why the argument is invalid

The quotation is exact word for word, which makes it convincing; it is the cut that misleads. The removed context (a concession, a hypothesis, irony) changes the meaning.

In practice

Example

A reviewer writes: “This film could have been a masterpiece if it had had a script.” The poster reads: “A masterpiece!”

Famous case

In On the Origin of Species (1859), Darwin writes that supposing the eye could have formed by natural selection “seems, I freely confess, absurd in the highest possible degree”. The sentence is often quoted alone, whereas Darwin immediately explains why this difficulty is only apparent, describing the intermediate stages observed in nature.

Defending yourself

How to respond

Go back to the full source and read what comes before and after. Ask whether the author would endorse the proposed interpretation.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Cherry-picking

Cherry-picking selects data from among others; quoting out of context cuts up a statement to change its meaning.

Going further

Source

The expression “quote mining” spread in debates over evolution, where quotations from biologists were taken out of context.
