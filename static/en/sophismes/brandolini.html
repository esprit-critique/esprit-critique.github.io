S 16Fallacy number 16,
fallacy · Evasion

Gish gallop

Brandolini’s law, Mitraillage d'arguments

Overwhelming the other party with a large number of weak or false arguments, faster than it is possible to answer them.

The essentials

Argument diagram

A puts forward N arguments in a few minutes.

B can refute only a few of them.

A concludes that the others stand unchallenged.

Why the argument is invalid

Quantity replaces quality. Refuting a claim takes far more time than making it, so the arguments left unaddressed wrongly seem to hold.

In practice

Example

In a televised debate, a speaker reels off fifteen unsourced figures in two minutes. His opponent spends his speaking time checking two of them.

Defending yourself

How to respond

Don’t try to refute everything. Dismantle the strongest argument, name the technique, and ask the other person to defend one point at a time.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Red herring

The red herring moves the debate to another subject; the Gish gallop floods it with quantity.

Going further

Source

The expression “Gish gallop” is Eugenie Scott’s, about the debates of the creationist Duane Gish. Alberto Brandolini (2013): “The amount of energy needed to refute bullshit is an order of magnitude bigger than to produce it.”
