Genetic fallacy

Sophisme génétique

Judging an idea true or false, good or bad, by its origin rather than its merits.

The essentials

Argument diagram

P comes from source S.

So P is false (or true).

Why the argument is invalid

The origin of an idea can explain why we believe it, but it does not determine its truth. An idea born in bad conditions can be right, and vice versa. The personal attack is a particular case.

In practice

Example

“This method was invented by a private company to sell its products: it can’t be reliable.”

Famous case

August Kekulé said he found the ring structure of benzene (1865) by dreaming of a snake biting its own tail. This dreamlike origin takes nothing away from the validity of the structure, established by other means.

Defending yourself

How to respond

Separate the history of an idea from its justification: “Its origin doesn’t tell us whether it is true. What lets us judge it today?”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Personal attack

The personal attack targets the person who defends an idea; the genetic fallacy, the historical origin of that idea.

Going further

Source

Hans Reichenbach (1938) distinguishes the context of discovery of an idea from its context of justification.
