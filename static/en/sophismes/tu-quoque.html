S 2Fallacy number 2,
fallacy · Personal attack

You too

Tu quoque, Appeal to hypocrisy, Toi aussi

Dismissing a criticism by pointing out that the person making it does not follow what they advocate themselves.

The essentials

Argument diagram

A claims P.

A’s behaviour contradicts P.

So P is false.

Why the argument is invalid

A person’s inconsistency concerns their conduct, not the truth of what they claim. A smoker who advises quitting is right about the risks of tobacco.

In practice

Example

“You blame me for eating too much sugar? You had three slices of cake yesterday.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Acknowledge the inconsistency if need be, then separate it from the question: “Perhaps, but that says nothing about whether it is true.”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Personal attack

The personal attack targets a person’s flaws in general; “you too”, the contradiction between their words and their actions.

Going further

Source

Douglas Walton, “Ad Hominem Arguments” (1998): tu quoque (“you too”) is one of the types of personal attack, which blames opponents for acting against what they argue.
