fallacy · Evasion

Red herring

Diversion, Hareng rouge

Introducing a subject unrelated to the question in order to divert attention from the point under debate.

The essentials

Argument diagram

Question: P?

Answer: here is some material on Q, which has nothing to do with P.

Why the argument is invalid

The discussion changes its subject without the original question having received an answer. The new subject is often chosen because it is more favourable or more emotional.

In practice

Example

Asked about a rise in local taxes, a mayor answers at length about the successful renovation of the municipal swimming pool.

Famous case

The “Chewbacca defense” from the series South Park (1998), a parody of Johnnie Cochran’s closing argument at the O. J. Simpson trial: the lawyer points out that Chewbacca lives on Endor, which “does not make sense”, and concludes that the jury must acquit.

Defending yourself

How to respond

Return explicitly to the question: “That’s a different subject. On the question asked, what is your answer?”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Straw man

The red herring changes the subject; the straw man stays on the subject, but distorts the opposing position.

Going further

Source

The English expression is said to come from an anecdote by the journalist William Cobbett (1807): a smoked herring dragged across the ground to throw hounds off a scent.
