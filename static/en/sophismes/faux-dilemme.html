False dilemma

False dichotomy, Black-and-white fallacy, Faux dilemme

Presenting two options as the only possible ones, when others exist.

The essentials

Argument diagram

Either A or B.

Not A.

So B. (Although C, D… exist.)

Why the argument is invalid

The reasoning “A or B; not A; therefore B” is valid, but its premise “A or B” is false if other options exist. The choice is artificially narrowed, often to one unacceptable option and the one we want to impose.

In practice

Example

“Either you are with us, or you are against us.”

Famous case

George W. Bush, speech to Congress of 20 September 2001: “Either you are with us, or you are with the terrorists.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Look for intermediate or different options: “Why only these two possibilities?”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Middle ground fallacy

The false dilemma artificially reduces the options to two; the middle ground fallacy assumes the truth lies halfway between two positions.

Source
