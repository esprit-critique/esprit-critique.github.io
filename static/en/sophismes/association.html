Guilt by association

Reductio ad Hitlerum, Déshonneur par association

Discrediting an idea because it is also defended by a person or group considered despicable.

The essentials

Argument diagram

X defends P.

X is beyond the pale.

So P is false.

Why the argument is invalid

The same idea can be supported by very different people for different reasons. That an odious group has defended a position says nothing about its truth: the Nazis were also opposed to tobacco.

In practice

Example

“You defend economic planning? That was also the policy of the USSR.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Separate the idea from those who carry it: “Who else defends it is not the question. What are the arguments against it?”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Personal attack

The personal attack targets the person who defends an idea; guilt by association targets other people, considered despicable, who also defend it.

Going further

Source

The expression “reductio ad Hitlerum” is Leo Strauss’s (1951). “Godwin’s law” (1990) is a humorous observation of the same tendency.
