fallacy · Causation

Single cause fallacy

Causal oversimplification, Cause unique

Attributing a complex phenomenon to a single cause, when it results from several factors combined.

The essentials

Argument diagram

B happened.

A is one of the causes of B.

So A is the cause of B.

Why the argument is invalid

A single explanation is easy to remember and points to someone responsible. But most social, economic or medical phenomena have multiple causes, which interact.

In practice

Example

“The French Revolution happened because of the price of bread.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Ask which other factors played a part, and how much each contributed. Be wary of explanations that point to a single culprit.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Post hoc ergo propter hoc

Post hoc draws a causation from mere succession; the single cause fallacy keeps one real factor but ignores all the others.

Going further

Source

Described in the logical tradition as the “single cause fallacy” or “causal oversimplification”.
