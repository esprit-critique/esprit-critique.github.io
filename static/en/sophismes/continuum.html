Continuum fallacy

Beard fallacy, Sorites paradox, Sophisme du continuum

Concluding that a distinction does not exist, or does not matter, because one passes from one to the other by imperceptible steps, with no sharp boundary.

The essentials

Argument diagram

Between A and B, there is no precise limit.

So A and B do not really differ.

Why the argument is invalid

That a boundary is blurred does not mean there is no difference: we do not know at how many hairs a beard begins, but a clean-shaven chin is not a beard. Thresholds (age of majority, speed limit) are useful conventions, even if they are arbitrary at the margin.

In practice

Example

“One grain of sand does not make a heap. Adding one grain to what is not a heap does not make it a heap. So heaps do not exist.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Accept that a distinction that is blurred at the boundary remains sharp at the extremes. Distinguish “where exactly should the threshold go?” from “do we need a threshold?”

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Slippery slope

The slippery slope predicts chained consequences; the continuum fallacy denies a difference because it is reached in small steps.

Going further

Source

The sorites paradox, or paradox of the heap, is among the arguments that Diogenes Laertius attributes to Eubulides of Miletus, of the Megarian school (Lives of Eminent Philosophers, Book II, 108).
