Persuasive definition

Redéfinition partisane

Redefining a value-laden word in order to apply it, or refuse it, to what one wants to praise or condemn, while keeping its emotional charge.

The essentials

Argument diagram

The word M has a positive (or negative) connotation.

M is redefined to mean X.

So X deserves the same approval (or disapproval).

Why the argument is invalid

The definition quietly changes the meaning of a word but keeps its prestige or its stigma. The conclusion seems to follow from the word’s usual meaning, when it depends on the new definition.

In practice

Example

“True freedom is obeying the rules I propose.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Ask which definition is being used and compare it with the ordinary sense. Ask whether the argument would hold with a neutral word.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Equivocation

Equivocation slides a word from one sense to another during an argument; the persuasive definition imposes a new sense from the outset on a value-laden word.

Going further

Source

Charles Stevenson (1938), “Persuasive Definitions”, in the journal Mind.
