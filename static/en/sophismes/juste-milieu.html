Middle ground fallacy

Argumentum ad temperantiam, Sophisme du juste milieu

Assuming that the truth necessarily lies halfway between two opposing positions.

The essentials

Argument diagram

A claims X.

B claims Y.

So the truth lies between X and Y.

Why the argument is invalid

A compromise can be useful in negotiation, but it guarantees nothing about the truth. If one person says 2 + 2 = 4 and the other that 2 + 2 = 6, the answer is not 5. This fallacy also favours whoever takes the most extreme position.

In practice

Example

“Some say the Earth is 4.5 billion years old, others 6,000 years. The truth is probably somewhere in between.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Judge each position on its evidence, not on where it sits between two extremes.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: False equivalence

The middle ground fallacy looks for the truth halfway between two positions; false equivalence presents two positions or two facts as equivalent.
