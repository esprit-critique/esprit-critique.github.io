fallacy · Evasion

Moving the goalposts

Déplacement des poteaux

When the evidence asked for is provided, demanding different or more demanding evidence, without acknowledging that the first request has been met.

The essentials

Argument diagram

A: “Show me X and I’ll admit it.”

B shows X.

A: “You also need Y.”

Why the argument is invalid

The standard of proof is never fixed, so the position becomes impossible to refute. Asking for more evidence can be legitimate; rejecting after the fact what had been judged sufficient is not.

In practice

Example

“Show me a transitional form between two species.” Once the form has been shown: “Now the form between this one and the next is missing.”

Defending yourself

How to respond

Have the person specify in advance what would count as sufficient evidence, then remind them of it once the evidence has been provided.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: No true Scotsman

Moving the goalposts changes the standard of proof demanded; the no true Scotsman move changes the definition of a group to dismiss a counterexample.

Going further

Source

An expression from sport: moving the goals during the match.
