Fundamental attribution error

Correspondence bias, Erreur fondamentale d'attribution

Explaining other people’s behaviour by their personality rather than by the situation, underestimating the weight of circumstances.

The essentials

Mechanism

The person is the centre of our attention, whereas the constraints weighing on them are barely visible. For our own actions, by contrast, the situation is obvious, hence an asymmetry.

In practice

Example

A driver who cuts us off is judged “irresponsible”, whereas when we do it, it is “because we were in a hurry”.

Famous case

Ross, Amabile and Steinmetz (1977): participants chosen by lot play the role of questioner, who invents difficult questions, or of contestant. Contestants and observers then judge the questioner to be more knowledgeable, without taking into account the advantage his role gave him.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Before drawing a conclusion about someone’s character, ask which constraints of the situation could explain their behaviour.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Self-serving bias

The fundamental attribution error concerns the explanation of other people’s behaviour; self-serving bias, that of our own successes and failures.

Going further

Source

Jones and Harris (1967): participants attribute a pro-Castro opinion to the author of a text favourable to Fidel Castro, even knowing that the position had been assigned to him. Lee Ross (1977) gave the phenomenon its current name.
