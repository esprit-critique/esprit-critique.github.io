False consensus effect

Projection bias, Effet de faux consensus

Overestimating the proportion of people who share our opinions, tastes or behaviours.

The essentials

Mechanism

Our own choices seem the most reasonable to us, and we mostly mix with people who are like us. Our circle is nonetheless not representative of the population.

In practice

Example

A devoted reader of a newspaper thinks that most of the population shares that paper’s editorial line.

Famous case

Ross, Greene and House (1977) ask students to walk around campus wearing an advertising sandwich board. Those who agree estimate that a majority of students would agree too; those who refuse, that a majority would refuse.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Replace intuition with measurement: survey, user testing, data. Ask whether your circle is representative.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Curse of knowledge

The false consensus effect concerns the opinions and tastes we attribute to others; the curse of knowledge, the knowledge we assume is shared.

Going further

Source
