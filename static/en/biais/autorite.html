Obedience to authority

Soumission à l'autorité

Carrying out the orders of an authority perceived as legitimate, even when they contradict one’s own values.

The essentials

Mechanism

People see themselves as the instrument of an outside will and transfer responsibility to the authority (the “agentic state”). Gradual commitment makes each new step hard to refuse.

In practice

Example

An employee applies a clearly abusive instruction because it comes from the hierarchy.

Famous case

Hofling et al. (1966): a fake doctor orders night-shift nurses by phone to give 20 mg of a fictitious drug, “Astroten”, whose label states a maximum dose of 10 mg. 21 nurses out of 22 prepare to do so before being stopped. A replication under more realistic conditions (Rank and Jacobson, 1977) finds only 2 out of 18.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Distinguish the legitimacy of a person from the legitimacy of a request. Put in place procedures that explicitly allow anyone, whatever their rank, to stop a dangerous action.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Appeal to authority (fallacy)

Obedience to authority consists of following orders; the appeal to authority, of believing a claim because an authority supports it.

Going further

Source

Stanley Milgram (1963). 65% of participants administered what they believed to be electric shocks of up to 450 volts to another participant, on the orders of an experimenter.
