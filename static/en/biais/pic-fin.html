Peak-end rule

Duration neglect, Règle du pic et de la fin

Judging a past experience mainly by its most intense moment and its end, with very little regard for its duration.

The essentials

Mechanism

Memory does not keep an average of the experience, but a few representative moments: the peak and the end. The total duration is almost ignored in the retrospective evaluation.

In practice

Example

A pleasant holiday whose last day is ruined by a cancelled flight can leave a generally bad memory.

Famous case

Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman (2003): patients whose colonoscopy is prolonged by a few minutes of lesser discomfort keep a less painful memory of the procedure, although they endured more discomfort in total.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Evaluate an experience from notes taken while it unfolds, not just from memory. Conversely, take care over the ending of an experience you provide for others.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Primacy effect

The primacy effect gives more weight to the first information received; the peak-end rule, to the most intense moment and the last moments of a lived experience.

Going further

Source

Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber and Redelmeier (1993), “When more pain is preferred to less”.
