Belief perseverance

Persévérance des croyances

Continuing to hold a belief as true after the information on which it was based has been discredited.

The essentials

Mechanism

Once a belief has formed, we build justifications and explanations for it. When the initial evidence disappears, those explanations remain and continue to support it.

In practice

Example

A rumour about a politician continues to influence public opinion even after a verified denial has been published.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

When a piece of evidence is discredited, ask explicitly: what else does my belief rest on? If nothing else supports it, give it up. Imagining explanations for the opposite conclusion reduces the effect.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Confirmation bias

Confirmation bias influences how we look for and read information; belief perseverance concerns the survival of a belief once its evidence has been discredited.

Going further

Source

Ross, Lepper and Hubbard (1975): participants told that they had done very well or very badly at telling real suicide notes from fake ones keep that impression, even after learning that their results were fictitious. Anderson, Lepper and Ross (1980) show that the effect is stronger when participants have explained the belief.
