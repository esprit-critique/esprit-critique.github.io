Anchoring effect

Anchoring, Effet d'ancrage

A numerical estimate remains influenced by a value presented earlier, even when that value is arbitrary.

The essentials

Mechanism

We start from the initial value and adjust it, but the adjustment is generally insufficient. The anchor also activates in memory information consistent with it.

In practice

Example

In a salary negotiation, the first figure put forward, by either side, strongly steers the final amount.

Famous case

Englich, Mussweiler and Strack (2006): experienced German judges read the file of a shoplifter, then roll loaded dice that land on 3 or 9. According to Daniel Kahneman’s account (2011), those who rolled 9 propose 8 months in prison on average, those who rolled 3 propose 5.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Make an independent estimate before learning the proposed figure. Ask whether the starting value has any justification.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Framing effect

Anchoring is the influence of a number presented before an estimate; framing is the influence of how the same information is worded (gain or loss, survival or mortality).

Going further

Source

Tversky and Kahneman (1974). A rigged wheel stops on 10 or 65. Participants are then asked to estimate the percentage of African countries in the UN: the median estimates are 25% in the first group and 45% in the second.
