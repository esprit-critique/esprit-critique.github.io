Curse of knowledge

Malédiction du savoir

Once you know something, it becomes hard to picture the point of view of someone who does not.

The essentials

Mechanism

Our knowledge imposes itself on our representation of the situation, and we cannot “switch it off”. We therefore overestimate what others know or understand.

In practice

Example

An expert teacher skips steps that seem obvious to them and cannot understand why the students are lost.

Famous case

Elizabeth Newton (1990): participants tap out the rhythm of a well-known song on a table and estimate that the listener will recognise it one time in two. In reality, fewer than 3% of tunes are recognised: the tapper hears the melody in their head, while the listener hears only knocks.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Have a beginner read it or test it. Spell out the “obvious” steps and define every term.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: False consensus effect

The curse of knowledge concerns what others know; the false consensus effect, what they think or prefer.

Going further

Source

Camerer, Loewenstein and Weber (1989) introduced the expression in economics.
