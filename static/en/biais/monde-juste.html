Just-world hypothesis

Just-world belief, Croyance en un monde juste

Believing that everyone gets what they deserve, and concluding that victims are partly responsible for their fate.

The essentials

Mechanism

Thinking that the world is just is reassuring: if misfortune strikes those who deserve it, we can protect ourselves by behaving well. Faced with an injustice we cannot repair, it is more comfortable to devalue the victim than to call this belief into question.

In practice

Example

“If he’s been unemployed for so long, it’s because he isn’t really looking.”

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Distinguish the cause of an event from a person’s fault. Remember that chance and circumstances play a large part in what happens to each of us.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Fundamental attribution error

The fundamental attribution error explains behaviour by personality; the belief in a just world explains a misfortune by the victim’s fault.

Going further

Source

Melvin Lerner and Carolyn Simmons (1966): participants who watch a young woman receive supposed electric shocks judge her more harshly when they cannot make her suffering stop. Melvin Lerner, “The Belief in a Just World” (1980).
