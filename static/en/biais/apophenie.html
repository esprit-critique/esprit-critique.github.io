Apophenia

Pareidolia (for images and sounds), Apophénie

Perceiving meaningful patterns or connections in random or unrelated data.

The essentials

Mechanism

The brain constantly detects regularities. According to a common evolutionary explanation, missing a real pattern, such as a predator in the grass, used to cost more than seeing one that does not exist: the system is therefore thought to be tuned to produce false positives.

In practice

Example

Seeing faces in clouds, electrical sockets or the fronts of cars.

Famous case

The “face on Mars”, photographed by the Viking 1 probe in 1976 in the Cydonia region. Higher-resolution images from Mars Global Surveyor (1998, 2001) show a simple eroded mesa.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Ask how many patterns you would have found in purely random data. Test the pattern on new data, without knowing in advance what you should find in it.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Texas sharpshooter fallacy (fallacy)

Apophenia is the spontaneous perception of a pattern; the Texas sharpshooter fallacy turns a pattern spotted after the fact into an argument.

Going further

Source

Term introduced by the psychiatrist Klaus Conrad (1958).
