Status quo bias

Default effect, Biais du statu quo

Preferring the current situation or the default option, even when an alternative would be more advantageous.

The essentials

Mechanism

Change involves effort, uncertainty and the risk of regret. Because of loss aversion, the drawbacks of change weigh more than its advantages.

In practice

Example

Keeping the same electricity supplier for years without ever comparing offers.

Famous case

Johnson and Goldstein (2003): in Germany, where people have to register to be organ donors, about 12% of the population consents. In neighbouring Austria, where consent is presumed, the rate exceeds 99%. This is registered consent, not the number of actual donations, which also depends on other factors.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Ask yourself: if I were starting from scratch, would I choose the current situation? Assess the default option like any other.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Loss aversion

Loss aversion is the mechanism; status quo bias is one of its manifestations: changing exposes us to losses that weigh more than the gains.

Going further

Source

Samuelson and Zeckhauser (1988). Johnson and Goldstein (2003): the rate of consent to organ donation varies by several dozen points between neighbouring countries, depending on whether consent is presumed or must be given explicitly.
