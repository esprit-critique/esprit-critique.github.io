Self-serving bias

Biais d'autocomplaisance

Attributing one’s successes to personal qualities and one’s failures to outside factors.

The essentials

Mechanism

This bias protects self-esteem. It is made easier by the fact that any result has several possible causes: we retain the ones that favour us.

In practice

Example

An investor credits their gains to their analysis and their losses to the “unpredictable volatility of the market”.

Famous case

Bettman and Weitz (1983), on shareholder letters in annual reports: good results are attributed mostly to internal factors (strategy, teams), bad ones to external factors (economic conditions, weather, regulation).

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Apply the same type of explanation to successes and failures. Ask what share belongs to chance or context in both cases.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Fundamental attribution error

Self-serving bias explains our own successes and failures in a flattering way; the fundamental attribution error explains other people’s behaviour by their personality.

Going further

Source

Miller and Ross (1975), a review of work on attributions of success and failure.
