Primacy effect

Effet de primauté

The first pieces of information received carry a disproportionate weight in the final impression.

The essentials

Mechanism

The first elements create an initial interpretation through which the following ones are read. They also benefit from better memorisation, because they receive more attention.

In practice

Example

In a job interview, the first few minutes steer the assessment of the rest of the exchange.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Defer your judgement until you have all the elements. In an assessment, use a grid fixed in advance and evaluate the criteria separately.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Halo effect

The primacy effect comes from the order of information; the halo effect, from the extension of a salient quality to other qualities.

Going further

Source

Solomon Asch (1946), experiments on impression formation (“intelligent, industrious, impulsive, critical, stubborn, envious” presented in one direction or the other).
