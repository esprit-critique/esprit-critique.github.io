Decoy effect

Asymmetric dominance effect, Effet de leurre

Adding an option that is clearly inferior to one of the existing options makes that option more attractive.

The essentials

Mechanism

Options are evaluated by comparison rather than in absolute terms. The decoy option, clearly dominated by a target option, provides an easy reason to choose the latter.

In practice

Example

Popcorn: small €3, medium €6.50, large €7. The medium mostly serves to make the large look like a bargain.

Famous case

Dan Ariely (2008) reports a subscription offer from The Economist: web $59, print $125, web + print $125. With these three options, 84% of his students choose web + print. Without the print-only option, which nobody takes, only 32% do.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Mentally remove the option nobody will choose and check that the choice stays the same. Start from your needs rather than from the comparison between offers.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Anchoring effect

The decoy effect works by comparison with an added option; anchoring, by a starting number that steers an estimate.

Going further

Source

Huber, Payne and Puto (1982).
