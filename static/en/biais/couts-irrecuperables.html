Sunk cost fallacy

Concorde effect, Biais des coûts irrécupérables

Persisting with a project or a choice because of the resources already invested, when only future costs and benefits should count.

The essentials

Mechanism

Giving up amounts to admitting waste, which is experienced as a loss. So we commit further to justify past investments, which are lost whatever we decide.

In practice

Example

Staying until the end of a boring film “because the ticket was paid for”.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Consider only the future: from today, which option offers the best ratio of remaining cost to expected benefit?

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Status quo bias

The sunk cost fallacy pushes people to persevere because of what they have already invested; status quo bias, to change nothing, even without any past investment.

Going further

Source

Arkes and Blumer (1985). Participants who had paid for two incompatible ski trips ($100 and $50) mostly chose the more expensive one, even knowing they would prefer the cheaper one. The expression “Concorde effect” comes from the continuation of the supersonic airliner programme despite its known unprofitability.
