Impact bias

Affective forecasting error, Biais d'impact

Overestimating the intensity and duration of the emotions a future event will cause, whether happy or unhappy.

The essentials

Mechanism

When imagining the event, we focus on it alone and forget everything else in life, which will carry on. We also neglect our capacity to adapt and put things in perspective, what Daniel Gilbert calls the “psychological immune system”.

In practice

Example

We imagine that a promotion will make us happy for years; a few months later, satisfaction has often returned to about its previous level.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Ask people who have already been through the event rather than imagining your own reactions. Think of everything in an ordinary day that will not change.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: Optimism bias

Optimism bias concerns the probability of events; impact bias, the intensity and duration of the emotions they will cause.

Going further

Source

Gilbert, Pinel, Wilson, Blumberg and Wheatley (1998), “Immune neglect”: in several studies (tenure denial, romantic break-up, election defeat), participants overestimate how long their emotional reactions will last. Wilson and Gilbert (2005), a review of affective forecasting.
