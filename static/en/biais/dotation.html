Endowment effect

Effet de dotation

Attaching more value to an object simply because you own it.

The essentials

Mechanism

Parting with an object is experienced as a loss, whereas acquiring it is experienced as a gain. Because of loss aversion, the selling price asked exceeds the price one would agree to pay.

In practice

Example

A used-car seller values their vehicle well above its book value, and finds the offers received “insulting”.

Famous case

Jack Knetsch (1989) gives one group a mug and another a chocolate bar, then offers each the chance to swap. 89% keep the mug and 90% keep the chocolate, whereas a third group, free to choose, splits roughly evenly. Plott and Zeiler (2005) showed that the size of the effect depends heavily on the procedure.

Defending yourself

How to protect yourself

Ask how much you would pay to acquire the object if you did not own it. That amount is a better estimate of its value.

Don’t confuse

≠ Not to be confused with: IKEA effect

The endowment effect arises from mere possession; the IKEA effect, from the effort invested in making or assembling.

Going further

Source

Kahneman, Knetsch and Thaler (1990). Students given a mug ask for a median of about twice what other students are willing to pay for the same mug.
